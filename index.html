What the bump actually is

Your thigh muscle pulls on your kneecap, the kneecap pulls on a tendon, and that tendon anchors into a small bump on the top of your shin. On a 12-year-old that anchor isn't finished bone yet — it's a growth plate, still soft and still building.

So the tendon is pulling hard on a construction site. That tug is the ache. It's called Osgood-Schlatter, roughly 1 in 10 sporty teenagers get it, and it isn't damage. It's an area being asked for more than it can currently give.

It can grumble on for a year or more and flares when you have a growth spurt. That's normal, and it isn't going backwards. The aim isn't a knee that never aches — it's playing a full season without ever having to stop.

Tell someone straight away if…

You feel a sudden pop while striking or landing, and afterwards you can't straighten your leg or put weight on it. Stop, don't walk it off, say so immediately. It's rare, and it's a different thing from this.
